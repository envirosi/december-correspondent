# From the editor’s desk

## Invitation email
**Subject — A small December publication of doubtful financial standing**

Dear friends,

On Tuesday we open The December Correspondent, a little Christmas paper with desks in the UK, Cebu and Sydney. Each morning there’ll be a door on the front page and another in your own pigeonhole. Your personal invitation is below. Please keep it to yourself, on the off chance somebody sends you something affectionate and ruins your reputation.

[Personal invitation link]

Read when you fancy. Miss a week and the back issues will still be there. If a caption occurs to you, send it in. If all that occurs to you is tea, that’s a perfectly sound editorial decision.

Inside there’s also a voluntary Secret Santa. A song, a GIF or a handwritten recipe counts. The spending limit is zero and Finance have been most particular on this point.

The paper closes on 31 January. After that everything in it, letters included, is deleted, so anything you write here won’t follow you into 2027.

Would whoever last saw the biscuit tin please get in touch.

Back to the studio,
Si — Junior Tree Correspondent at ETN

## Quiet request for a personal letter
I’m putting a little post behind one of [name]’s December doors. Can you think of a particular moment this year when they made something easier, kinder or funnier for you? One or two sentences in your own words would be lovely. Please say how you’d like to sign it. It goes privately to [name]. I’ll ask again if we ever want to print it.

## Pouch copy
**Join the Diplomatic Pouch** · Fancy sending one person a virtual present? A song and a sentence explaining why you picked it make a complete parcel. No wish lists, no shopping. Enrol by [local deadline]. You can change your mind until names are drawn.
**The spending limit is zero** · Every parcel is virtual and free: a few words, plus a link if you like. Please don’t buy anything, send vouchers or ask anyone for money.
**Need an idea?** · A song that makes you think of them · a GIF that sums up their year · a short video that made you laugh · a photo you took this year · a recipe from your corner of the world · a free recommendation · a handwritten note, photographed · the imaginary present you’d give with an unlimited budget · a very specific compliment.
**After the draw** · You’re sending a little post to [name]. Keep it secret, keep it kind, keep it free. Aim to seal it by 17 December so it arrives on the 18th.
**On sealing** · Wax applied. Thumb briefly attached. Your parcel is ready.
**Reserve parcel, prepared by the editor** · A Christmas gift from the picture desk. Your imaginary memoir has been commissioned under the title “I Was Muted for the Interesting Bit”. The paperback will include a foreword by a man still trying to share his screen. With affection from the December desk.

## Working dates · proposed for 2026
- 26–27 November · Confirm reader list and regional desks. Ask one colleague privately for each recipient’s note. Record the preferred signature. Prepare reserve gifts.
- 30 November · Rehearse with fictional readers. Editor checks roster, invitation links, local dates and private notes. Issue individual invitations once the rehearsal passes. Earliest door opens at 20:00 UK time that evening in Sydney.
- 1 December · Open the edition. Editor reviews incoming contributions on working days. Relay lines on days 3, 17 and 21 publish straight away so the story can cross time zones overnight; the editor can still take one down. The edition reads properly even if nobody sends anything.
- 8 December · Close enrolment at 09:00 UK / 17:00 Cebu / 20:00 Sydney. Editor runs the draw. Show assignments in each giver’s pouch.
- 11 December · Check note coverage. If a note is missing, ask someone who knows the reader or write a genuine one yourself. Target day 14 for delivery. A later day also works.
- 17–18 December · Check sealed parcels and prepare individual reserves. Open parcels on 18 December at 07:00 local time. People on leave can collect theirs later.
- 14–16 December · Check private thank-you notes sent through door 14 and release them to their recipients.
- 23–24 December · Select willing contributors’ moments for the final edition. Confirm quoted wording and permission. Publish the closing story even if there are no submissions.

## Editor’s voice
Let the Correspondent take the pratfall. Use colleagues’ own words for kindness. Invite local detail without asking anyone to speak for a country. Keep jokes about office life understandable without UK Christmas trivia. Use captions for sounds and pictures. Offer text as a complete contribution. A quiet day needs no editorial intervention.

- 31 January · Close the paper. Run the purge so letters, parcels, contributions and the reader list are deleted.
